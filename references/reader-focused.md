# Reader-focused review

Use this after syntax and structural checks when the request is to improve a
document for people rather than merely make it valid.

- Lead with what the reader can accomplish or understand.
- Use descriptive headings and familiar words; define necessary project terms.
- Keep overview pages conceptual. Link to schemas, exhaustive rule indexes,
  protocol details, and contributor procedures where they become relevant.
- Avoid brittle inventory claims such as “six tools” when the exact count does
  not help a reader choose or complete a task.
- Put prerequisites before instructions and keep each procedure in a usable
  order. State consequential defaults and safety boundaries near the action.
- Prefer one clear explanation over repeated summaries. Remove navigation links
  that duplicate nearby routes without serving a distinct reader need.
- Keep examples realistic and small enough to scan. Explain the result or choice
  they demonstrate instead of exposing incidental implementation detail.
- Preserve the writer's voice. Flag subjective rewrites and terminology changes
  for approval rather than silently normalizing them.

Finish by reading the rendered target in sequence. Lint and compatibility tools
can establish correctness, but not whether the page answers a reader's question
clearly and without unnecessary effort.
